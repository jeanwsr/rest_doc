# First-Order Skeleton Derivatives of the DFT Fock Matrix

This document discusses the formulas and implementation for computing the first-order skeleton derivatives of the DFT Fock matrix. We use only the matrix multiplication strategy, without any other optimization tools.

:::{note}
**This document treats only the closed-shell case**

For open-shell systems, part of the computational procedure has to be reworked by spin components. We will discuss this issue in other documents.

## 1. First-Order Skeleton Derivatives of the Fock Matrix: Overview

We first recall the definition of the Fock matrix. The Fock matrix is the first derivative of the energy with respect to the density (as variational parameters):

$$
V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc} = \frac{\partial E^\text{xc}}{\partial D_{\mu \nu}}
$$

For some practical considerations, a factor-of-2 scaling may be introduced in this derivative for the closed-shell case. The above equation holds for all energy contributions; of course, here we discuss only DFT grid integration.

Substituting the DFT energy expression

$$
E^\text{xc} = \sum_g w_g (f \rho)_g
$$

we obtain the grid-integration representation of the Fock matrix:

$$
V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}
= \sum_{\chi g} w_g \frac{\partial (f \rho)_g}{\partial \xi_g^\chi} \frac{\partial \xi_g^\chi}{\partial D_{\mu \nu}}
= \sum_{\chi g} w_g f_g^\chi \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi
$$

Now we take one more derivative, but not with respect to the density matrix; rather, we differentiate the Fock matrix with respect to the atomic coordinates. We note that the terms with an explicit dependence on the atomic coordinates comprise two parts: the functional kernel $f_g^\chi$, and the representation $\xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi$ of the basic DFT quantities under the basis set.

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} = \sum_{\chi g} w_g \frac{\partial f_g^\chi}{\partial A_t} \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi + \sum_{\chi g} w_g f_g^\chi \frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi}{\partial A_t} \quad (\texttt{vmat\_deriv1})
$$

In the above equation, the former produces the second derivative of the functional kernel, $f_{g}^{\chi \chi'}$, and is therefore denoted `vmat_fxc`; the latter involves only the first derivative of the functional kernel, $f_g^\chi$, and is therefore denoted `vmat_vxc`. We will discuss the formulas and implementation of these two parts separately.

:::{warning}
**Missing grid-shift derivative contributions**

The discussion above holds for the ideal case of integration over the full spatial coordinates. In practice, it indeed gives a reasonable Hessian in quite a few cases (referring to hybrid LDA/GGA functionals).

However, as a numerical integration scheme, we have not taken into account the effect of the atomic-coordinate changes on the grid points $\boldsymbol{r}_g$ and the weights $w_g$. Hessian calculations for meta-GGA generally require the grid-shift contributions to be included. We will discuss this issue in other documents.
:::

## 2. Implementation Details of the `fxc` Part

### 2.1 Overview of the `fxc` Contribution Implementation

The central problem of the `fxc` contribution is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} &\leftarrow \sum_{\chi g} w_g \frac{\partial f_g^\chi}{\partial A_t} \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi
\quad (\texttt{vmat\_fxc}) \\
&= \sum_{\chi \chi' g} w_g f_g^{\chi \chi'} \frac{\partial \xi_g^{\chi'}}{\partial A_t} \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second-order skeleton derivatives of the energy we have already computed `drho` $\partial_{A_t} \xi_g^{\chi'}$, the derivative of the density on grids with respect to the coordinates; the simplification in the above equation is therefore already the final form.

### 2.2 Concrete Implementation of the `fxc` Contribution

In the concrete implementation, first note that the weights $w_g$, the second derivative of the functional kernel $f_g^{\chi \chi'}$, and the derivative of the density on grids with respect to the coordinates $\partial_{A_t} \xi_g^{\chi'}$ do not involve the atomic orbitals $\mu, \nu$. These quantities can first undergo an inexpensive contraction over the index $\chi'$, yielding the four-dimensional intermediate:

$$
\mathscr{T}_{g \chi}^{A_t} = \sum_{\chi'} w_g f_g^{\chi \chi'} \frac{\partial \xi_g^{\chi'}}{\partial A_t}
\quad (\texttt{wf\_rho})
$$

In the actual implementation, **we adopt a strategy that iterates in the outer loop over the atom index `A` and the three-dimensional coordinate $t$, which turns the present problem into one exactly equivalent to ordinary Fock matrix construction**; in other words, the only difference is that we generate $3 n_\mathrm{atom}$ matrices, but each of them is no different from a Fock matrix in terms of program implementation.

**`vmat_fxc`: LDA (RHO)**

First perform the element-wise scaling to obtain `aow`; since we are inside the loop over the indices `A, t`, `aow` is a 2-dim tensor $(g, \mu)$. Also note that $\chi$ has only a single component, $\rho$, so the $\chi$ index can be omitted.

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t} = \mathscr{T}_{g}^{A_t} \phi_{g \mu} \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

The matrix multiplication can then be carried out directly, giving the derivative of the Fock matrix with respect to the atomic coordinates:

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} \leftarrow \sum_{g} \mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t} \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\texttt{vmat\_fxc}, \text{LDA})
$$

Merely for the convenience of the subsequent program implementation, a symmetrization with respect to $(\mu, \nu)$ is introduced here, hence the factor of 0.5:

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} \leftarrow \frac{1}{2} \sum_{g} \mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t} \phi_{g \nu} + \mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu) \quad (\texttt{vmat\_fxc}, \text{LDA})
$$

Since the tensor with the smaller memory footprint during the computation is `wf_rho` $\mathscr{T}_{g}^{A_t}$, a trick is to apply the factor scaling directly on `wf_rho`, rather than scaling during the computation or on the result.

**`vmat_fxc`: GGA (SIGMA) / MGGA (TAU)**

We first give the straightforward derivation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} 
&\leftarrow \sum_{g} \mathscr{T}_{g, \chi = \rho}^{A_t} \phi_{g \mu} \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\text{RHO}) \\
&\quad + \sum_{g} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} \mathscr{T}_{g, \chi = \rho^r}^{A_t} (\phi_{g \mu}^r \phi_{g \nu} + \phi_{g \mu} \phi_{g \nu}^r) \quad (\text{SIGMA}) \\
&\quad + \sum_{g} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} \mathscr{T}_{g, \chi = \tau}^{A_t} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \phi_{g \mu}^r \phi_{g \nu}^r \quad (\text{TAU})
\end{aligned}
$$

We need to exploit a coincidence: there is a correspondence between the density components $\chi \in \{ \rho, \rho^x, \rho^y, \rho^z \}$ (`nvar = 4`) and the atomic-orbital derivative components $* \in \{ 1, x, y, z \}$ (`ncomp = 4`). Based on this coincidence, and also considering the $\mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu)$ symmetrization to be introduced shortly, we first re-determine the scaling coefficients of `wf_rho`:

$$
\bar{\mathscr{T}}_{g, \chi}^{A_t} = \begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2} \mathscr{T}_{g, \chi}^{A_t} & \chi = \rho \\
\mathscr{T}_{g, \chi}^{A_t} & \chi = \rho^r, r \in \{ x, y, z \} \\
\frac{1}{4} \mathscr{T}_{g, \chi}^{A_t} & \chi = \tau 
\end{cases}
$$

**For the GGA case**, we make full use of this coincidence by contracting in advance the index $\chi \in \{ \rho, \rho^x, \rho^y, \rho^z \}$ with the corresponding $* \in \{ 1, x, y, z \}$, constructing the 2-dim temporary tensor `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t}$ of shape $(g, \mu)$:

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t} = \sum_{\substack{\chi \in \{ \rho, \rho^x, \rho^y, \rho^z \} \\ * \in \{ 1, x, y, z \}}} \bar{\mathscr{T}}_{g \chi}^{A_t} \phi_{g \mu}^* \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

Then perform the simple matrix multiplication:

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} \leftarrow \sum_{g} \mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t} \phi_{g \nu} + \mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu) \quad (\texttt{vmat\_fxc}, \text{GGA})
$$

**For the MGGA increment case**, we need to loop over the three cases $r \in \{ x, y, z \}$, constructing for each the temporary tensor `aow`, $\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t r}$, of shape $(g, \mu)$:

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t r} = \bar{\mathscr{T}}_{g, \chi = \tau}^{A_t} \phi_{g \mu}^r \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

Then perform the incremental matrix multiplication:

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} \leftarrow \sum_{g} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} \mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^{A_t r} \phi_{g \nu}^r + \mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu) \quad (\texttt{vmat\_fxc}, \text{MGGA increment})
$$

## 3. Implementation Details of the `vxc` Part

### 3.1 Overview of the `vxc` Contribution Implementation

The central problem of the `vxc` contribution is

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} \leftarrow \sum_{\chi g} w_g f_g^\chi \frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi}{\partial A_t} \quad (\texttt{vmat\_vxc})
$$

In the treatment of this term, the application of $\delta_{\mu \in A}$ is carried out at the very end. We first produce an intermediate matrix `vmat_ip`, which resembles a derivative with respect to the electronic coordinates. This is similar to the situation of `dao_vxc_diag/off` in the second-order skeleton derivatives of the energy.

We first need to convert the nuclear-derivative $\partial_{A_t}$ problem into an electronic-coordinate-derivative $\partial_{t}$ problem (borrowing the relation $\partial_{A_t} \phi_{g \mu}^* = - \partial_t \phi_{g \mu}^* \delta_{\mu \in A}$). For convenience in the subsequent treatment, we define the temporary tensor $\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^{t}$, which is richer in detail and partial-differentiated only with respect to the basis-set index $\mu$. Note that this tensor is **not symmetric in $\mu, \nu$**.

$$
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^{t} = \sum_{\chi g} w_g f_g^\chi \frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^\chi}{\partial t} \quad (\text{restrict $\partial$ to $\mu$}, \texttt{vmat\_ip})
$$

Then the contraction with respect to the atom index $A$ gives the final vxc contribution:

$$
\frac{\partial V_{\mu \nu}^\text{xc}}{\partial A_t} = - \mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^{t} \delta_{\mu \in A} + \mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu)
$$

### 3.2 Concrete Implementation of the `vxc` Contribution

The implementation structure of the `vxc` contribution is in fact similar to that of the Fock matrix; one only needs to note two differences from the `fxc` case:
- The intermediate quantity of the `vxc` contribution, $\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^{t}$, is not symmetric in $(\mu, \nu)$, so a little more care is needed when writing the code.
- In the $\xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\rho^r}$ ($r \in \{x, y, z\}$) constructed for GGA (SIGMA), the derivative components of the bra $\phi_{g \mu}^r$ and of the ket $\phi_{g \nu}$ are not symmetric, so some extra code implementation is required relative to the Fock matrix calculation.

`vmat_ip` has no dependence on the atom index at all: the 3 matrices for $t \in \{x, y, z\}$ only need to be generated once; the dependence on the atom index `A` is deferred entirely to the contraction process involving $\delta_{\mu \in A}$ inside `get_vmat_vxc`.

**`vmat_ip`: LDA (RHO)**

The LDA part corresponds to $\chi = \rho$, with $\xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \rho} = \phi_{g \mu} \phi_{g \nu}$. Differentiating with respect to $\mu$ only,

$$
\frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \rho}}{\partial t} = \phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu} \quad \text{(restrict $\partial$ to $\mu$)}
$$

Since we differentiate with respect to $\mu$ only, the derivative index $t$ is fixed on the bra, so the element-wise weighting is applied to the ket; first perform the element-wise scaling to obtain the 2-dim temporary tensor `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \nu}$ (note that it carries the ket index $\nu$, unlike the `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}$ of `fxc`; for LDA the resulting matrix is the same on whichever side the weighting is applied, and the ket is chosen so as to be consistent with the first contraction structure of GGA):

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \nu} = w_g f_g^{\rho} \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

Then perform the matrix multiplication for each $t$:

$$
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t = \sum_{g} \phi_{g \mu}^t \mathscr{W}_{g \nu} \quad (\texttt{vmat\_ip}, \text{LDA})
$$

**`vmat_ip`: GGA (SIGMA)**

The GGA task corresponds to the superposition of the two parts RHO + SIGMA. Differentiating with respect to $\mu$ only,

$$
\frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \rho}}{\partial t} = \phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\text{RHO}), \qquad \frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \rho^r}}{\partial t} = \phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu}^r + \phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\text{SIGMA})
$$

Substituting into the definition of $\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t
&= \sum_{g} w_g f_g^{\rho} \phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\text{RHO}) \\
&\quad + \sum_{g} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} w_g f_g^{\rho^r} \left( \phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu}^r + \phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \phi_{g \nu} \right) \quad (\text{SIGMA})
\end{aligned}
$$

Of the two SIGMA summation terms, the first, $\phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu}^r$, has its two derivative components on the bra and on the ket respectively, while the second, $\phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \phi_{g \nu}$, has both derivative components on the bra. Hence the exact one-to-one overlap of $\chi \leftrightarrow *$ in `fxc` cannot be applied directly here, and the contraction cannot be completed with a single `aow` tensor as in Section 2.2. Still, the expression above can be decomposed into two matrix multiplication structures.

The first structure applies the element-wise weighting to the ket. The temporary tensor `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \nu}$ does not depend on the index $t$ and can be hoisted outside the $t$ loop:

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \nu} = \frac{1}{2} w_g f_g^{\rho} \phi_{g \nu} + \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} w_g f_g^{\rho^r} \phi_{g \nu}^r \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

$$
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t \mathrel{+}= \sum_{g} \phi_{g \mu}^t \mathscr{W}_{g \nu}
$$

This structure covers the first SIGMA term, $\phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu}^r$, as well as half of the RHO term.

The second structure applies the element-wise weighting to the bra. The temporary tensor `aow_d` $\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^t$ depends on the index $t$, and its $\phi_{g \mu}^{t r}$ is taken from the second-derivative components of `ao`:

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^t = \frac{1}{2} w_g f_g^{\rho} \phi_{g \mu}^t + \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} w_g f_g^{\rho^r} \phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \quad (\texttt{aow\_d})
$$

$$
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t \mathrel{+}= \sum_{g} \mathscr{W}_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu} \quad (\texttt{vmat\_ip}, \text{GGA})
$$

This structure covers the second SIGMA term, $\phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \phi_{g \nu}$, as well as the other half of the RHO term.

The index structure of the RHO term, $\phi_{g \mu}^t \phi_{g \nu}$, is compatible with both structures, so it is split evenly with a $\frac{1}{2}$ factor into each of the two structures, and the total factor is restored to 1 after summation. Note that the motivation for the $\frac{1}{2}$ here is completely different from that in `fxc` (Section 2.2): the scaling coefficients $\bar{\mathscr{T}}$ of `fxc` serve the $\mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu)$ symmetrization, whereas `vmat_ip` performs no symmetrization inside the function, and the $\frac{1}{2}$ exists purely to fold the RHO term into the two matrix multiplication structures above, avoiding a separate matrix multiplication just for the RHO term.

At the program level, this case reads `ao` up to the second-derivative components ($\phi$, $\phi^r$, $\phi^{t r}$, i.e. `ncomp = 10` in the table of Section 3.1). The computational cost is $6 \times 2 n_\mathrm{basis}^2 n_\mathrm{grid}$ FLOPs, i.e. two structures each performing 3 matrix multiplications over $t$.

**`vmat_ip`: MGGA (TAU) increment**

The MGGA increment corresponds to $\chi = \tau$, with $\xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \tau} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} \phi_{g \mu}^r \phi_{g \nu}^r$. Differentiating with respect to $\mu$ only,

$$
\frac{\partial \xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \tau}}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{r \in \{x, y, z\}} \phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \phi_{g \nu}^r \quad \text{(restrict $\partial$ to $\mu$)}
$$

We need to loop over the three cases $r \in \{x, y, z\}$, constructing for each the 2-dim temporary tensor `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \nu}^r$:

$$
\mathscr{W}_{g \nu}^r = \frac{1}{2} w_g f_g^{\tau} \phi_{g \nu}^r \quad (\texttt{aow})
$$

Then perform the incremental matrix multiplication:

$$
\mathscr{T}_{\mu \nu}^t \mathrel{+}= \sum_{g} \phi_{g \mu}^{t r} \mathscr{W}_{g \nu}^r \quad (\texttt{vmat\_ip}, \text{MGGA increment})
$$

The $\frac{1}{2}$ here is not an implementation trick, but the coefficient inherent in the definition of $\xi_{g \mu \nu}^{\chi = \tau}$; in contrast, in the MGGA increment of `fxc` the factor is $\frac{1}{4}$ because of the additional $\mathrm{swap} (\mu, \nu)$ symmetrization (the product of the inherent $\frac{1}{2}$ and the symmetrization $\frac{1}{2}$). The loop order takes $r$ as the outer loop and $t$ as the inner loop, so that `aow` $\mathscr{W}_{g \nu}^r$ depends only on $r$ and is reused in the inner loop. The computational cost is an additional $9 \times 2 n_\mathrm{basis}^2 n_\mathrm{grid}$ FLOPs, i.e. $3 \times 3 = 9$ matrix multiplications over $r \times t$.
